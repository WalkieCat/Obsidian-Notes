## How do you protect information:
- You need to know yourself, your system - mechanisms and methods to protect the information, and your enemy - threats
- The primary mission for info security is for these information to remain safe, else if the threat is not present then resources can be used to improve system that contain, use and transmit info
- This process perform 4 important functions:
	- Protect the ability to function
	- Protect the data used and collected
	- Enable safe operating of IT systems
	- Safeguard technology assets
- Business usually wins when collaborating with security needs and business needs

## Safeguard technology assets:
- Orgs must use appropriate infrastructure assets
- This may develop when the orgs grow

## Threats and attack:
- Threats are any potential risks to your information assets, attacks are ongoing acts targeting the assets that could result in a loss
- Threat agents are those who seek an [[Attack]], [[Exploit]] or [[Vulnerability]] to steal an org assets by utilizing/taking advantage of a control that is not longer efficient

## Compromise on Intellectual Properties
- These can be trade secrets, copyrights, trademarks and patents
- IP is protected with various international laws, and users are expected to credit or acquire permission to use in accordance with law. Otherwise it can be considered an attack on information security
- This concerns two primary category:
	- Software piracy
	- Copyright protection 

## Deviation in quality of service
- The success of an org's information system is dependent on various other systems working flawlessly together, of which can all be affected by unforeseen events
- Other failure points can be network service irregularities, communications and power supply issue

## Espionage/Trespass:
- Espionage refers to an unauthorized user trying to gain access to an org's protected information
- When this process involves technique that pass a certain legal threshold, it is called industrial espionage
- A hacker might spend hours examining the system's structures and defense using various techniques to attempt to bypass controls and authentication
- When access is granted, the hacker may perform privesc attacks to gain further access
- Another type of espionage is password attacks, concern several types such as brute-force attacks, dictionary attacks, rainbow tables etc.

## Forces of nature:
- The most dangerous threat as it does not only concern information security but also human lives, transport, storage and use of information

## Human error/failure:
- Concerns intentional/unintentional acts performed by an authorized user when using information system
- Some of these failure point includes inexperience, lack of training and incorrect assumptions
- One of the biggest threat concerning information security is its own employee as they are the closet to the information
- Some typical errors are:
	- Social engineering
	- Fraudulent 
	- Phishing

## Information extortion:
- The act of stealing information from an organization that is then exploited for monetary gains
- This has evolved into malware, where the malicious software encrypts the user data and offer to decrypt them for a price
