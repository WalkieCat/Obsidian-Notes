
# Enterprise system business functions
Organizational structure are dependent upon the company's target, environment, current use of technology and future objectives (Donaldson 2001, Vaskun and Koczkás 2024). To better understand how different businesses might use a different structure based on their needs, the paragraphs below discuss the structure of a service-intensive industry such as Local Government, and a retail/manufacturing sector such as Woolworths.

## Service-intensive industry
**Structure**: Built to address the need of the voters
In the service industry, the primary objective of the organization is to use their resources in such a way that it satisfy the needs of the voters that built the council. Since the voters holds the power to rebuild the org at will, most of the effort of the council will be dedicated to resource management in such a way that the decision of the council is reflective of the voters.

## Retail sector
**Structure**: Built to address the need of the stakeholders
Similarly speaking, Woolworths also answer to their stakeholders but with a different objective. Since Woolworth is committed to maximize the profit margin of their stakeholders, they will appoint a board of directors that oversee most of the operations in the companies. This board will then appoint executives who can manage different departments of the organization. Thus, most of the effort of the organization is directed to ensure that all stakeholders receive the most profit, by focusing more on efficiency.

## Main difference
The main difference for each sector is their objective of their organization. Once the organization have a clear objective in mind, their resources will either be restructured or redirected in a way that the objective remains at the forefront of the company. For this example, the local government main objective is reflective of the voter's need, so their effort my be focused on managing their resources so that these tasks are done. On the other hand, Woolworths' objective is to maximize the profit margin of their stakeholders, so their focus on efficiency is to address this objective.

# TechnologyOne Case Study
## Types of configuration elements 
The four types on configuration elements presented by Rachel Butler were:
1. Foundation: This is the element that once they are formed should not be changed since they have a large impact on the organization structure. Example: Chart of account design 
2. Industry regulation driven: Industry specific template that governs the element within a template. Example: A system pre-configured to calculate lease liabilities to comply with international standards
3. Industry common practice: Common elements of the industry that forms a template based on proven practice processes. Example: A system configured to approve an invoice only 
4. Customer specific: Specific configuration made for a customer. Example: A payment system for a client's business

## Streams
Streams are the grouping of Functional Groups with an accelerated template designed to help support key business areas through different implementation phases. An example of a stream is the Financial Management stream.

The Financial Management stream is made up of the following groups:
1. General ledger: Forms the foundation of the template with multiple subsidiary ledger within a chart of account 
2. Chart of accounts: A tool with all financial accounts in the general ledger which provides information about financial transactions within a unique period
3. Assets accounting
4. Taxation
5. Banking
