**BACKGROUND**
RIC - or Regional Investment Corporation is an Australian Government provider of low interest loans to farm businesses and other farm-related businesses. Its purpose is to support the growth, resilience and sustainability of the Australian agricultural economy. RIC is also working towards developing the improvement of regional Australian farm despite geological differences. RIC head office is based in Orange, Central West Regional of NSW, with most of its work force distributed across Australia (Regional Investment Corporation 2025)

RIC was established by the Regional Investment Corporation Act of 2018 to streamline the delivery of $4 billion dollars in loan. Initially, the RIC set up two schemes for farm businesses and National Water Infrastructure Loan Facility (NWILF) (Australian National Audit Office 2025). As of 2023, the RIC has delivered more than 2,900 loans to different farms and businesses, valued at over $3.17 billion dollars (Technology One 2023), and currently has $464.9 million dollars available for funding in FY 2026 (Regional Investment Corporation 2025). Recently, the Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry has approved two new loan scheme to RIC to help famers combat drought and help recover from marine related incidents. Furthermore, the DAFF also approved an additional $1 billion dollars in new loan funding for the RIC, and expanded its scope to include improving climate resilience, boosting productivity and supporting agriculture to become a net zero economy (Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry 2024)

**OVERARCHING BUSINESS MOTIVATION**
The RIC core mission to help distribute the concessional loans to help ease the financial burden on farmers requires the organization to be fast and accurate simultaneously. This mission created several non-negotiable requirements:
- Address all the farmers in need quickly and effectively in times of hardship. The reliance on manual reporting poses an issue for this requirement, since disruption arrives quickly and suddenly, demanding RIC to response in a timely manner.
- Since RIC is reporting to two Government regulations, it must satisfy the obligations set out by both regulations. Disconnected systems make this process harder to control and more prone to user errors.
- With the Australian Government broadening the scope for the RIC, it must be able to scale to meet with new demands that come along with new responsibility. Its system must be able to scale without proportionately increasing administrative cost as the demands grow. 

**INITIAL SITUATION**
The RIC was built to purposefully manage the loan approval process instead of state or the government processing. Initially, paper forms, emails and spreadsheets proved useful to help the organization to get to running quickly has grew obsolete and needed to evolve (Technology One 2023). This proves to be hard to maintain since the introduction of low-interest loan has attracted thousands of farmers and businesses to apply, leading to increased response and approval time for applications. With around 63 staff spread across Australia, RIC had no formal HR management system in place to properly manage their growing staffs. Additionally, their existing HR, Finance and Payroll department operates separately from each other with numerous back and forth communications. Without an integrated system in place, communications between these departments often lead to data duplication and over-reliance on manual reporting, thus increasing the burden on the organization (Technology One 2023).

**OVER-RELIANCE ON MANUAL PROCESSING AND LACK OF INTEGRATED SYSTEM**
Without an integrated HR system, there was duplication of data and reliance on manual reporting. In this practice, employees need to manually maintain the data that is existing in different places. HR might maintain some data that Finance do not have and have to rely on Payroll for the data, and vice versa. This leads to redundant communication between department and no autonomous data syncing with other departments.

Prolonged internal communication of just one department can lead to the creation of information silos for these departments. These silos can be detrimental to RIC, as they often only share information between their respective department, and thus could not relay real time information to management to make key decisions. This can leads to hindered communications and cooperation (Jones et al. 2024). Furthermore, relying on manual review can overload personnel with processes, which will increase the chance for human errors. These errors will have to be checked and amend manually by other reviewer, thus increasing the time needed to effectively manage information (Nasrudin, Nurin Izzdhinie & Mohamad 2025). The implementation of ERP is described as a packaged software solution to integrate the range of a business processes and functions to present a single, united view of the business on a single interface (Klaus, Rosemann & Gable 2000). This integration is necessary if RIC wants to improve its operation and eliminate data entry error and rely less on manual review. 

Furthermore, the absent of a managed HR system, all employees record and data are funneled through the central HR system. This created a dependency where employees are dependent on HR for all administrative tasks, of which can be delegated to more important processes. This pattern was framed by Godwin (2023) as the main characteristic of an organization that has yet to leverage the capabilities of an ERP to automate tasks where possible, and focus more on activities that brings in more capital.

**POTENTIAL SOLUTIONS**
Option 1: Continue with operation
RIC can make small changes to its existing infrastructure, which saves on the enormously upfront cost but does not solve the underlying problems. As RIC expands further, more loans requests, staffs and policy will be needed, which can bring the total administrative cost to a much higher degree. Nasrudin and Mohamad (2025, p. 12) note that organizations continuing to rely on manual processes as they expand do not simply inherit the same problems at larger scale. Rather, their problems actively worsen, as error rates and reconciliation burdens grow non-linearly with complexity.

Option 2: Purchase dedicated tools 
RIC could purchase dedicated tools for each of its department to reduce the reliance on manual processing. It could then integrate this via a middleware, either made in house or another specialized software license. While this would help with the data processing, it recreates the problem at a higher level. This approach introduces the integration and maintenance costs, and still would require the flow of data coming from different department and not a single source of data. 

**WHY ERP**
An integrated ERP addresses all of these problem simultaneously and allows for modular implement as RIC grows even further. Godwin (2023) argues that for smaller businesses, having an ERP proportionately outweighs the cost because it delivers a single united system, compare to what would have required multiple vendors, integration software and redesigning workflow to fully integrate its system. 

Technology One's solution in particular was well-suited because it is purpose-built for Australian government entities. The results at RIC confirm the choice. With Technology One's Human Resources & Payroll seamlessly integrated in real-time with Financials, it has created one source of employee data that enables RIC to profile their workforce. The organization can now get real-time analysis and reporting, making performance management a much easier task.
