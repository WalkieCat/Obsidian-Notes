**BACKGROUND**
RIC, or Regional Investment Corporation, is an Australian Government provider of low-interest loans to farm businesses and other farm-related businesses. Its purpose is to support the growth, resilience and sustainability of the Australian agricultural economy. RIC's head office is based in Orange, Central West Regional of NSW, with most of its work force distributed across Australia (Regional Investment Corporation, 2025).

The RIC was established under the Regional Investment Corporation Act of 2018 to help deliver the $4 billion in loans to farmers in need under two schemes, namely the farm business concessional loan and the National Water Infrastructure Loan Facility (NWLIF) (Australian National Audit Office, 2025). As of 2023, the RIC has delivered more than 2,900 loans to different farms and businesses, valued at over $3.17 billion dollars (Technology One, 2023). As of 2025, the RIC has $464.9 million dollars available for funding in FY 2026 (Regional Investment Corporation, 2025). Recently, the Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry has approved two new loan schemes to help farmers combat droughts and recover from marine related incidents. Additionally, the RIC also received another $1 billion in funding to expand its scope to include improving climate resilience, boosting productivity and supporting the agricultural industry to become net-zero (Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry, 2024). RIC has grown significantly from its original mandate, and continues to take on greater responsibility within the Australian agricultural sector.

**OVERARCHING BUSINESS MOTIVATION**
With the demand for loans from farmers and added funds from the government, expanding scope and loan schemes proposed by the government, and its inherent duty as a legislation organization, RIC will struggle to upkeep its operation. The manual reporting system that was created since its creation can no longer reasonably scale up to accommodate the responsibility of the organization as it grows.

At its core, RIC was created as a way to streamline the loaning process for thousands of farmers. It is responsible for managing $4 billion in loan, along with another $1 billion from the Australian Government under new loan schemes. These loans are to serve as a means to relieve the financial pressure on farmers and businesses in hardship. Should these loans not reach these farmers, a massive backbone of the Australian GDP will collapse entirely. Financial hardships, especially natural disasters, affect farmers without prior notice. Therefore, the RIC also has responsibility to respond in a timely manner to these events. According to reports, the agriculture industry alone contributed to 2.2% of the Australian economy from 2024 to 2025 (Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry 2024). These loans are crucial for farmers to restart their operations after a disaster or financial hardship, and also a critical lifeline for an industry that underpins the Australian economy.

The RIC mission scope is increasing rapidly, with the new loan schemes introduced by the Australian government. It is necessary for RIC to realize that their existing manual setup will no longer be sufficient. Additionally, with the government pushing further to combat climate resilience and steering the agriculture industry to become a net-zero industry, the RIC continues to be given additional pressure on top of its existing framework. These changes necessitate the change in how RIC functions internally for it to be able to handle these new scope changes.

Furthermore, since RIC was created under the Australian Commonwealth legislation, it has a duty to report to and be held accountable under the Australian government. It primarily reports to two ministers: Minister for Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, and the Minister of Finance. Therefore, it has a duty to be compliant to all legislation and guidelines that is suggested by the government, and will be subjected to an investigation should it be found to not be compliant, or in breach of certain legislation. Compliance becomes harder with the RIC still relying on manual reporting, which makes it harder for RIC itself to gather enough evidence to show that it is compliant with the government standard. 

**INITIAL SITUATION**
The RIC was built to purposefully manage the loan approval process instead of state or the government processing. Initially, paper forms, emails and spreadsheets proved useful to help the organization get running quickly. However, this setup has since grown obsolete and needed to evolve as the organization grew (Technology One, 2023). This proves to be hard to maintain since the introduction of low-interest loan has attracted thousands of farmers and businesses to apply, leading to increased response and approval time for applications. With a larger volume of applicants, manual system takes time to verify each of the application, which leads to a slower approval time. This can directly hurt the farmers who urgently require these loans to rebuild their livelihood from the ground up.

Internally, RIC is also struggling with a problem. With around 63 staff spread across Australia, RIC had no formal HR management system in place to properly manage their growing staffs. Without a centralized system, it becomes increasingly harder to manage employees who are geographically scattered. Information relating to these employees, such as records, payroll and HR actions cannot be verified in person. Without a centralized system, RIC must verify every employee detail manually, adding significant time to an already stretched team. Additionally, their existing HR, Finance and Payroll department operates separately from each other with numerous back and forth communications. This leads to each department having their own records, and no single department has a complete picture of the entire data flow of the organization. These departments will then possibly enter the same data multiple times, and without an automatic system in place, inconsistencies start to emerge. Without an integrated system in place, communications between these departments often lead to data duplication and over-reliance on manual reporting, thus increasing the time that the 63 staff are spending on administrative tasks, whereas it could be spent more thoughtfully on urgent tasks such as verifying and approving loan applications (Technology One, 2023).

**OVER-RELIANCE ON MANUAL PROCESSING AND LACK OF INTEGRATED SYSTEM**
The problems identified above are symptomatic of a deeper reliance on manual processing and a fundamental lack of integration across RIC's internal systems. Prolonged internal communication of just one department can lead to the creation of information silos for these departments. These silos can be detrimental to RIC, as they often only share information between their respective department, and thus could not relay real time information to management to make key decisions. This can lead to hindered communications and cooperation (Jones et al., 2024). The formation of these silos means that all departments operate on their own set of data, and no single department has the complete picture of the entire organization. This makes it harder for leadership to make a decision for what loan scheme to focus more on, since they have to gather data from multiple sources to avoid making decisions based on incomplete data.

Furthermore, relying on manual review can overload personnel with processes, which will increase the chance for human errors. These errors will have to be checked and amended manually by other reviewers, thus increasing the time needed to effectively manage information (Nasrudin, Nurin Izzdhinie & Mohamad, 2025). Beyond efficiency, this reliance on manual reporting creates a direct compliance risk for the organization. The RIC must demonstrate at any time that it can meet the compliance standard outlined by the government such as maintaining accurate loan records, maintaining a financial report of all given loans and an audit trail that could be independently verified by a third party. Thus, any human errors made during the evidence gathering process can be a failure point for the entire organization. Inaccurate records can lead to the RIC not being determined as compliant, and lead to the RIC being investigated. A compliance failure means that RIC also fail to uphold the very lifeline that farmers depend on. 

The implementation of ERP is described as a packaged software solution to integrate the range of a business processes and functions to present a single, united view of the business on a single interface (Klaus, Rosemann & Gable, 2000). This integration is necessary if RIC wants to improve its operation and eliminate data entry error and rely less on manual review. With an ERP, the RIC could address all of its issues within one singular system. This system will help resolve information silos, allowing departments to communicate and exchange data with each other. A single interface reduces the possibility of data being entered multiple times, thus reducing duplication of data. This combines into a unified view of the entire data flow, which helps leadership come up with decisions about loan schemes that will affect thousands of farmers.

In the absence of a managed HR system, all employees' records and data are funneled through the central HR department. This creates a dependency where employees are dependent on HR for all administrative tasks, of which can be delegated to more important processes. This pattern was framed by Godwin (2023) as the main characteristic of an organization that has yet to leverage the capabilities of an ERP to automate tasks where possible, and focus more on activities that bring in more capital.  This will eliminate the administrative tasks that the 63 staff are required to do manually, such as verifying HR records, and focus their effort on more important tasks related to the RIC core mission of helping farmers in need.

Given these accumulated problems, RIC initiated a formal procurement process to find a suitable ERP vendor, which ultimately led to the selection of TechnologyOne as the ERP vendor.
